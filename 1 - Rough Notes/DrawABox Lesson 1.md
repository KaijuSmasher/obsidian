# Using the different pivots of the arm

The arm is made up of three major joints and when we want to manipulate an object in space with it we can bend the arm along the following pivots:
- Wrist
- Elbow
- Shoulder

The range of motion that is achievable is dependent on which pivot of the arm we chose to use.
When drawing only with the wrist pinned in place it enables us to make precise and tight strokes, but makes longer strokes pretty much impossible. It's good for when very tight, stiff control is needed, like for drawing and tight details.

![[Pivots of the Arm.jpg]]

When drawing with the whole arm, where everything, including the upper arm, is moving as you draw. It's great for smooth, fluid linework, even for smaller marks, because it allows us to achieve straight and clean strokes over longer trajectories and it minimizes wobbling. The shoulder is the most important pivot of the arm when it comes to drawing and it's very important to use it to improve the flow and cleanliness of the strokes we want to make.
When using the shoulder to draw it's important to not anchor the elbow on the table because it locks the range of movement we would otherwise have. It's okay to land the side of the hand or a finger gently rest on the page, as long as you don't slip back into using the wrist.

# How to hold the pen
There are two kinds of grip one of which is the overhand grip which allows us to use all sides of, for example, a charcoal/led pencil allowing a broad variety of strokes. It's also pretty good to force us into using the shoulder since the wrist is more locked up while holding a pencil this way.

The other one is the tripod grip which is used for tools like mechanical pencils that only have the tip so only one dimension of variability. It's the way we are used to holding pencils because it's the grip we are used to for writing.
